### Article 1 (full draft): How to donate to a children's home in Kenya

**Slug:** /how-to-donate-to-a-childrens-home-in-kenya/
**SEO title:** How to Donate to a Children's Home in Kenya (2026 Guide)
**Meta description:** Practical steps to give money, food or supplies to a children's home in Kenya safely: how to check an organisation, pay by M-Pesa or card, and avoid common mistakes.

The safest way to donate to a children's home in Kenya is to give to an organisation you can verify, through an official payment channel in the organisation's own name, and to ask what is actually needed before giving goods. This guide explains how to do that, whether you live in Kenya or abroad.

**1. Check who you are giving to**
- Look for a physical address, a working phone number and a website or official page that has been active for some time.
- Ask what the organisation is registered as (for example a Community Based Organisation, NGO or Charitable Children's Institution) and with which office.
- Be cautious of appeals that only use a personal M-Pesa number or ask for urgent cash for an individual child.
- Ask how donations are used and whether reports or updates are shared.

**2. Choose how to give**
- *M-Pesa Paybill (Kenya):* the quickest option. Check the Paybill and account number on the organisation's own website.
- *Card or online payment:* useful for donors outside Kenya. Make sure the payment page belongs to the organisation.
- *Food and supplies:* call first. Children's homes often have specific needs (for example staple foods, school supplies or hygiene items) and limited storage.
- *Regular giving:* a small monthly gift often helps more than a one-off, because it lets the home plan meals and school fees.

**3. Respect children's privacy**
A good children's home will not let donors photograph children, share their names or post their stories online. This protects children; it is not a sign of secrecy.

**4. Visiting to donate**
Agree a time in advance and follow the home's rules. Short, planned visits disrupt children's routines less than drop-ins.

### Article 2 (full draft): What to consider before volunteering at a children's home in Kenya

**Slug:** /volunteering-at-a-childrens-home-in-kenya-what-to-consider/
**SEO title:** Volunteering at a Children's Home in Kenya: What to Consider
**Meta description:** Thinking of volunteering with children in Kenya? What responsible children's homes ask of volunteers, why short-term placements are limited, and how to help well.

Volunteering with children in Kenya can do real good, but only when it fits what the children and the home need. Responsible children's homes limit volunteer numbers, check volunteers carefully and focus on skills rather than short visits. Here is what to consider before you apply.

**Why placements are limited**
Children who have experienced loss do best with stable relationships. A steady stream of short-term visitors can unsettle them. Kenya's care reform also encourages family- and community-based care, so homes increasingly focus on support that strengthens families and schooling.

**What a responsible home will ask of you**
- To be 18 or over and to provide references or a police clearance certificate.
- To follow staff guidance and never be alone with a child out of sight of staff.
- Not to photograph, film or post about children, or share their names or locations.
- Not to give children personal contact details or contact them outside the home.

**Skills that help most**
Teaching and tutoring, mentoring, counselling (if qualified), sports coaching, proposal writing and record keeping, and fundraising.

**Before you travel**
Do not book flights until the home has confirmed a placement in writing. Ask what costs you will cover and what the home expects.

**Other ways to help without travelling**
Fundraise among friends, give monthly, or offer professional skills remotely.

**Volunteering with Smile Community Centre**
Smile considers volunteer opportunities periodically, depending on current needs, capacity and safeguarding requirements. [Ask about volunteering](/volunteer/).

*Sources:* Kenya National Care Reform Strategy for Children 2022–2032.

### Article 3 (full draft): How companies can support children's welfare in Kenya (CSR)

**Slug:** /how-companies-can-support-childrens-welfare-in-kenya/
**SEO title:** How Companies Can Support Children's Welfare in Kenya (CSR)
**Meta description:** A practical guide for Kenyan and international companies planning CSR for children: choosing a partner, what to fund, staff involvement and safeguarding.

Companies can make a lasting difference for vulnerable children in Kenya by partnering with a verified local organisation, funding a clear, recurring need such as meals or school fees, and involving staff in ways that respect children's privacy.

**1. Choose a partner you can verify** — registration, physical address, contacts, track record, and willingness to report on use of funds.
**2. Fund something specific and recurring** — a term's school fees, a month of meals, hygiene kits, or a facility repair; agree what update you will receive.
**3. Involve staff responsibly** — planned group visits, skills days (finance, IT, proposal writing), and fundraising challenges; no photos of children on company social media without the partner's written consent.
**4. Think long term** — multi-year commitments let a children's home plan.
